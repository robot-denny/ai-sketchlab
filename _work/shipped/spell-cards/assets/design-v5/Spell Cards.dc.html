What changed in this pass

The layout is now fluid. Tiles and cards hold a ratio instead of a fixed height, and both scale their own interiors from their column width, so the stack row fills the space it is given and the grid falls to one card at a time on a phone without a single layout breakpoint. The one media query in the file drops the root type from 18px to 17px below 400px. Narrow the window to watch it move; the frames in the narrow reference are the target.

Selection now carries all the colour and all the motion. An open stack rises, lights its accent, wears an OPEN strip and points at the panel below it; the closed stacks fall back to a quiet dark plane with a still sigil. The deck beneath each top card became paper edges rather than more near-black, so the stack reads as objects on a z-axis instead of one flat shape.

Every sigil now takes its accent from its own pack, so the signal red belongs to Core alone. The face rule is gone. Each of the fourteen spells has its own drawn sigil, and every reference in every pack shares one static tome — kind is legible before you read a word.

The flat grid and its filter chips are gone. The deck now presents one stack per pack — the row above is the entire toolkit at a glance — and opening a stack reveals its cards sectioned into spells and references. The five deck dividers became the stacks themselves; their prose is the panel header you read on the way in.

The card is unchanged: same box, same 690px height, same flip, same reverse. What did change is the face. The reverse's code-versus-language split now runs on the front too — a spell wears its invocation in IBM Plex Mono on the deepest plane with a filled badge, a reference wears its name in Cormorant Garamond on a raised plane with an outlined one. Three signals, no new tokens, so a grid of sixteen reads as two kinds without a filter.
